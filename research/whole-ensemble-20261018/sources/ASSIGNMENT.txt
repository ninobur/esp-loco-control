1. Why the previous experiment must be repeated

Your architectural explanation established that the previous experiment did not test the recognition mechanism the operator requested.

The implementation classified independently selected waveform windows. It did not recognize complete magnetic ensembles, establish their physical boundaries, identify associated lobes, or recognize the intervening baseline as part of a continuous MM–BL–MM pattern.

Consequently, the reported 571 matches demonstrated correspondence between selected windows and provisional MM annotations—not recognition of 571 complete magnetic ensembles.

The 70-count experiment eliminated many false detections, but it did not correct this architectural deficiency.

We now want you to implement the actual recognition mechanism and repeat the comparison.

Do not repair the previous sliding-window classifier. Replace it.

2. Governing recognition concept

The Hall tracing contains alternating recognizable structures:

Baseline → Magnetic ensemble → Baseline → Magnetic ensemble

Each magnetic ensemble includes:

* Its principal positive or negative excursion.
* Associated leading and trailing lobes.
* Shoulders and other related waveform features.
* The transition from baseline into the magnetic field.
* The return from the magnetic field to baseline.

A baseline is a recognizable, relatively level portion of the tracing. It may curve gently, including during locomotive deceleration.

The fundamental navigation unit is:

MM₁ → BL → MM₂

Successive units overlap:

MM₁ → BL → MM₂ → BL → MM₃

Each physical magnet must be recognized as one ensemble and counted once.

The model must recognize these structures as wholes, not construct them from independently accepted or rejected waveform fragments.

3. The statistical recognition models

Repeat the original comparison using a corrected recognition mechanism.

Model A — General composite

Construct a statistical model describing the general class of magnetic ensembles from all 171 Lowline magnets.

Construct a corresponding baseline model.

The model must learn the relationships among principal excursions, associated lobes, shoulders, and baseline transitions.

Model B — Geographic-specific composite

Construct statistical models from repeated recordings of individual magnets MM050–MM070 and their associated baselines.

Use the same recognition mechanism as Model A.

The difference between A and B must be the statistical reference, not the detector, preprocessing, or evaluation method.

Determine whether individual geographic magnetic portraits provide a meaningful advantage over one general magnetic model.

4. Required recognition behavior

The corrected recognizer must:

1. Recognize principal magnetic hills and valleys as characteristic shapes.
2. Identify associated lobes and shoulders as belonging to their magnetic ensemble.
3. Recognize baseline regions independently, including gently curving baselines.
4. Establish the beginning and end of each complete ensemble through its transitions to and from baseline.
5. Establish the corresponding baseline boundaries.
6. Discount isolated spikes and insignificant baseline variations without treating them as magnets.
7. Determine the principal polarity of each ensemble.
8. Recognize the complete MM–BL–MM relationship.
9. Preserve the overlapping relationship between successive units.
10. Count each physical magnetic ensemble once.

These are interrelated recognition responsibilities, not ten independent qualification gates.

The recognizer must evaluate the continuous Hall tracing, not merely select independent windows that resemble magnetic fragments.

Recognition timing

RVM is retrospective.

The recognizer may wait until the complete magnetic ensemble has been observed before establishing geographic progression.

Do not optimize for earliest possible recognition.

5. Amplitude and waveform shape

Amplitude is meaningful evidence.

The previous experiment failed partly because normalization made tiny baseline fluctuations resemble full-sized magnetic excursions.

However, the 70-count requirement is an experimental finding, not a universal definition of a magnet.

Do not simply retain the 70-count threshold as an independent admission gate.

Instead, construct a statistical recognition model that considers amplitude together with waveform shape and surrounding baseline.

A legitimate magnetic ensemble should be recognized because its complete structure resembles the magnetic class, not merely because its principal excursion exceeds a fixed numerical threshold.

Preserve ordinary variation in amplitude and waveform breadth.

Do not use speed estimates or predicted magnet arrival to qualify recognition.

6. Exclude stationary magnetic recordings

New requirement: Exclude magnetic observations recorded while the locomotive was stopped from both statistical model construction and recognition-accuracy evaluation.

A stationary Hall sensor may remain within a magnetic field, producing a sustained signal that does not represent traversal of a magnetic ensemble.

Such observations must not distort the learned magnetic shape.

For this experiment:

* Identify stationary intervals using the recorded operating state and available movement evidence.
* Exclude stationary portions from the training and evaluation datasets.
* Do not interpret a stationary magnetic level as a complete magnetic ensemble.
* Do not create artificial ensemble boundaries by joining waveform portions across excluded stationary intervals.
* Preserve the excluded observations in the source inventory.
* Report the number and duration of excluded intervals and the evidence used to identify them.

Important: PWM zero alone does not prove the locomotive is stationary, and nonzero PWM does not prove movement. Use the available recorded evidence to distinguish confirmed stationary intervals from uncertain movement.

Movement evidence may be used only to determine dataset eligibility, never as an input to the magnetic recognizer.

If a recording’s movement status cannot be established, mark it uncertain rather than silently treating it as moving.

10. Success criterion

The experiment succeeds only if the recognizer demonstrates that it can identify complete magnetic ensembles and intervening baselines in continuous Hall recordings.

Matching arbitrary candidate windows to provisional MM annotations is insufficient.

The recognizer must demonstrate that it recognizes the characteristic magnetic field, including its associated lobes and return to baseline, as one physical ensemble.

It must distinguish that structure from the baseline and recognize the overlapping MM–BL–MM progression.

Then compare whether the general 171-magnet composite or the geographic-specific MM050–MM070 models accomplish that task more reliably.

Final instruction

We have repeatedly demonstrated that a collection of individually reasonable numerical qualifications does not necessarily produce reliable magnetic recognition.

Do not build another system that detects fragments and then tries to determine which fragments should count.

Build a statistical recognizer that understands the characteristic structure of an entire magnetic field and its relationship to the surrounding baseline.

That is the experiment we intended to conduct.
