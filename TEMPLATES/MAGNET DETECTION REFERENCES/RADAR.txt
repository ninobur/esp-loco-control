Cross-validation (encouraging, not new)
All three external sources (Gemini, the MAD summary, and the Grok/TANSE brief) independently converge on the same four techniques as primary candidates — matched filtering, CFAR-style adaptive thresholding, kinematic/measurement gating, sequential evidence accumulation — and all three independently flag full multi-hypothesis tracking (MHT) / particle filtering as unsuitable for the same reason I gave: targets here are 1-D, fixed, and one-at-a-time, so the combinatorial machinery radar/sonar need for many maneuvering targets has no problem to solve here. Four independent research passes landing on the same shortlist is a reasonably strong signal that this is the right toolkit to prototype against, not an artifact of one source copying another.
New leads worth chasing
1. Differential/gradiometer Hall sensing — a hardware, not just software, lead. The MAD summary's point about spatially-separated Hall elements rejecting common-mode background (Earth's field, traction EMI) while retaining a localized gradient is real, established magnetometry practice, and it's the one technique across all sources that current QUORUM/TEMPLATES thinking hasn't touched — everything in the interview record is single-Hall-sensor. Whether a second Hall element is physically worth adding is a hardware/cost question, not one this research assignment should answer, but it's worth surfacing as an option that exists.
2. The "refractory/dead-time lockout" framing directly names your open event-closure question. Gemini's "Clutter & Duplicate Rejection (Dead-Time/Refractory)" — borrowed from radar blind-zone logic — is really just a formal name for exactly what interview Q8/Q9 left unresolved: how long does one magnet's excursion count as "still the same event" before a new candidate can open. It doesn't answer the open question, but it does supply a standard vocabulary and a design pattern (a distance- or time-proportional lockout keyed to current speed, rather than a fixed sample count) for when you do collect the oscilloscope data needed to answer it.
3. A concrete, checkable sampling-rate red flag. Two of the three documents (Gemini and MAD) independently raise a Nyquist-type concern: the sample rate has to resolve the magnet's transit time, and transit time shrinks as speed rises (t = d/v). I checked this against numbers already in this repo rather than leaving it abstract:
docs/QUORUM_v3_0_implementation_spec.md:834 records 290.8 mm/s at PWM 100 as an observed locomotive speed (repo fact) — and the interview record's PWM test plan goes up to PWM 120, so top speed is higher still.
The interview record's own finding (Q9) is that existing HallProbe logs run at only ~9–10 samples/sec (repo fact).
I could not find a documented physical magnet size/field-extent in this repo, so I can't finish this calculation precisely — but even a generous 20 mm effective field zone at 290.8 mm/s gives a transit time around 69 ms, against a ~100–111 ms sample period. That's under one guaranteed sample per transit at PWM 100, before PWM 110–120 makes it worse.
This is an inference from repo facts, not a source-established fact — but it's a specific, testable number, and it directly reinforces item 1 in my earlier "smallest experiments" list (the high-rate Hall oscilloscope, Q10B/Q23) as a hard blocker rather than a nice-to-have: at the current sampling rate, matched filtering, SPRT, and excursion-shape work are arguably not yet meaningful regardless of which architecture is chosen.

Not new, lower-value
Wavelet/time-frequency analysis and orthonormal-basis-function MAD filtering (from the MAD summary) are real established techniques, but both assume you're extracting a weak signal from a noisy continuous background (airborne submarine detection) — a harder problem than a close-range Hall sensor passing directly over a known magnet. Worth knowing they exist; I wouldn't prioritize them.
The "1/r³ dipole falloff" physics (MAD summary) is a legitimate established fact and explains why flux depends so strongly on air-gap/alignment (relevant to the interview's Q7 flux-gate discussion), but it's background physics, not a technique to prototype.
