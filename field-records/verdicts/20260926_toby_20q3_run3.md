## Operator interventions and contamination

- **Only after stopping did the operator manually tilt the IR car to verify
  placement. Subsequent IR movement from that intervention is not locomotive
  travel.** Toby's applied PWM reached 0 at 09:43:18.6; no Hall event followed
  the stop.
- No intervention during the moving run is recorded.

## Outcome

**NAVI's position was wrong by two MMs for the entire reverse run, and nothing
corrected it.** Every Hall observation was a real magnet, and every IR distance
was consistent. The error was in identity.

- Physical sequence, derived from the observed opening poles against the map:
  the 32 accepted CW observations are **MM022 → MM053, 32 of 32 poles matching**.
  That agrees with the operator's final ground truth (MM053).
- NAVI labelled them **MM020 → MM051**: 17 of 32 poles matching, 15
  `ADVANCED_WITH_DISCREPANCY`. NAVI finished at **MM051**, two behind.
- No alignment other than MM022 → MM053 matches more than 21 of 32.

### How the two-MM error arose (from the log and the code)

1. Run 2's #30 (manual-handling contamination) left NAVI at MM020, one MM
   ahead, while Toby was on MM21.
2. At a reversal the navigator expects the point just passed as the first
   landmark in the new direction (inherited 0.4/0.5 design: `target = navMm`).
   NAVI therefore expected MM020. Toby was stopped **over** MM21, so leaving it
   in reverse produced no new opening; the first magnet actually detected was
   MM022, accepted Hall-only (no MM reference after the stop) as "MM020", with
   its S pole agreeing with MM020 by coincidence.
3. Each contribution is one MM: the inherited false advance, and the
   repeated-landmark expectation failing when the loco stops on the magnet.
   Even without step 1, this reversal would have left NAVI one behind.

### Why recovery did not correct it

`diag/recovery`: WARMUP for the first ten new-direction positions (#31–#40),
then `TRAVEL_UNAVAILABLE_HOLD` for every remaining event, with
`reference_mm:-1`, `travel_mm:null` and `possible_mask:0`. The incumbent scored
5–6 of 10; the physically correct +2 alternative, which would score 10 of 10,
was never examined. PROXIMAL_R1 clears its travel reference at a reversal and
re-establishes it only when mature history fully agrees with the incumbent,
which cannot happen while the incumbent is wrong. With no reference,
established recovery holds. This is the recorded PROXIMAL_R1 limitation
(physical gating depends on its own travel reference; decisional-inertia audit
gaps), now observed on track. It is not a 20Q3 change.

## What worked

- **Distance windows:** all IR-judged MMs were inside ±15% (0.858–1.126) at
  speeds up to about 300 mm/s (throttle up to 99–100). As expected, distance
  cannot detect an identity offset: the spans are all about 300 mm.
- **Recovery chain again:** epoch 9 ended 09:42:35.5 during braking (brake
  commanded 09:42:31–34); epoch 10 began 09:42:44.4; #60 accepted Hall-only,
  resynchronized, then #62/#64 judged by IR distance.
- **IR rejected three false Hall events that timing alone would not all
  catch:**
  - #61: +174, 402 ms and 19.3 mm after #60;
  - #63: −197, 402 ms and 9.7 mm after #62;
  - **#65: −70 (window peak −70, at threshold), 720 ms and 19.3 mm after #64,
    at PWM 24 while stopping over physical MM053.** The 650 ms Hall-only rule
    alone would have **accepted** this one. It has the same signature as run 2
    #30 (threshold-level re-detection on the magnet the loco is stopping on),
    this time with valid IR, which rejected it.
- **X22R baseline re-lock at speed:** 29 locks accepted, 3 rejected (one each
  `Moving`, `Cadence`, `Spread`). At cruise intervals of about 1 s the
  per-interval lock works; the run 1/2 cadence rejections were a slow-running
  effect.

## Anomalies and findings for review (recorded; no change proposed here)

1. **Sustained two-MM identity error after a reversal from a stop on a magnet**,
   uncorrected by recovery (above). In AUTO, station logic acts on `navMm`, so
   stops would have been two MMs off.
2. **Repeated-landmark expectation at reversal** assumes the last-passed magnet
   will be detected again. It was not, because Toby was stopped over it.
3. **Recovery holds without a travel reference after reversal**, even with 15
   contradictions and a unique fully-consistent alternative within ±10.
4. **Threshold-level re-detection while stopping on a magnet** now seen twice
   (run 2 #30 with IR blind → accepted; run 3 #65 with IR valid → rejected).
   This bears on run 2's creep-versus-handling question: creeping onto a
   magnet at low PWM can produce such an event without handling.
