Here is the summary I would give CODEX. The important point is that the “20 questions” exercise was not an attempt to create twenty special-case rules. It was how we forced ourselves to expose the physical relationships NAVI had previously been glossing over.

Purpose

The extended “twenty questions” discussion was a design exercise intended to make NAVI’s decision process explicit before changing firmware.

The central lesson was:

Do not solve individual anomalous Hall/IR events with additional symptom-specific rules. Define what each source actually measures, establish the physical constraints, and let NAVI apply explicit rules to the evidence.

The architecture remains:

DETECTION → HARD/PHYSICAL CONSTRAINTS → NAVI JUDGMENT

The discussion substantially clarified what “NAVI judgment” means.

⸻

1. NAVI maintains a world view

NAVI has a current believed position supported by:

* the operator’s starting declaration;
* accepted MM history;
* direction;
* surveyed MM map;
* measured travel;
* recent observations.

That existing position has decisional inertia.

One missed magnet, one wrong polarity, or one contradictory observation does not erase the accumulated evidence and create global ambiguity.

NAVI is where NAVI believes NAVI is until a preponderance of evidence supports a better physically possible explanation.

⸻

2. Possible before probable

When NAVI investigates an apparent positional discrepancy, it must first determine which alternative positions are physically possible.

Only then should it ask which possible position best matches the MM polarity evidence.

A mathematically excellent DNA/polarity match at an unreachable location is not a candidate.

This is why the present whole-route 171-position pattern search is conceptually wrong.

The absolute correction-search boundary is ±10 MM from NAVI’s incumbent position, but NAVI should not mechanically search all twenty alternatives. IR travel, direction, route history, PWM/motion evidence and other physical constraints should remove impossible candidates first.

±10 is an outer boundary, not a candidate list.

⸻

3. Rolling physical history

NAVI should retain approximately:

10 MM positions / approximately 3,000 mm of recent physical travel history.

This is not necessarily ten successful Hall observations.

Within that physical history:

* observed polarity remains evidence;
* wrong polarity remains evidence;
* a genuinely missed magnet is UNKNOWN/inconclusive;
* correction does not erase the observations;
* reversal should not unnecessarily destroy useful history.

Autonomous position reassignment should not occur before approximately this amount of history exists after session startup.

⸻

4. Position correction

After impossible alternatives have been eliminated, compare the incumbent and remaining physically viable candidates against the actual available evidence.

For polarity matching:

* missed MM = no polarity evidence;
* observed N/S = evidence, even if it disagrees with expectation;
* compare against however many actual polarity observations exist in the ~3 m history.

Decision rule:

* incumbent best → retain incumbent;
* one physically possible alternative uniquely better than incumbent → reposition;
* equal best alternatives → non-decisional; retain incumbent.

No arbitrary additional scoring margin was approved.

After a correction, retain the history. If the correction proves wrong, later evidence can correct it again.

⸻

5. IR’s role was fundamentally clarified

IR is an instrument, not an agent and not a navigator.

The useful analogy is a bicycle cyclometer.

IR answers:

“We have moved this much.”

NAVI, using MM landmarks and its map, answers:

“This is where we are.”

Do not give IR authority to identify MMs or diagnose locomotive behavior.

Likewise, lack of new IR pulses does not mean IR stopped providing data.

Fresh valid packets + unchanged wheel count means:

movement = 0.

That is valid data.

NAVI determines what zero means from locomotive state, PWM, Hall progression, station state and history.

⸻

6. IR health, readiness, odometry and MM synchronization are separate concepts

The emerging C++ architecture deliberately separates four facts:

Instrument health

Is the IR apparatus/data path functioning?

Checks include packet integrity/freshness, optical contrast, saturation, sampling continuity, counter/order integrity, calibration/pitch, etc.

Measurement readiness

Can wheel movement actually be observed at this instant?

For example, the current detector’s PRIMING and REACQUIRING states return before normal pulse processing. The instrument can be healthy while measurement is temporarily not ready.

IR odometry

How much wheel movement has actually been measured?

This exists independently of the MM map.

MM synchronization

Which known MM is the odometry referenced to?

At an accepted MM detection, NAVI associates:

MM identity + Hall detection timestamp + corresponding IR count

Only then can NAVI say “X mm from MMnnn.”

⸻

7. IR Epoch

We adopted the term IR Epoch:

An uninterrupted interval of valid IR odometry during which IR can account for all wheel movement continuously.

A normal stop, zero movement or station dwell does not end an IR Epoch.

Anything capable of permitting unmeasured/corrupted wheel movement ends the epoch.

Examples include genuine measurement loss, sampling failure, inadequate contrast while movement could occur, reboot, etc.

Critical invariant:

AN ENDED IR EPOCH NEVER REOPENS.

When measurement becomes ready again, a new IR Epoch begins immediately.

IR can therefore immediately resume saying:

“We have moved this much from this new origin.”

But it cannot reconstruct distance across the blind interval.

An MM distance reference is bound to the IR Epoch in which it was established. It cannot cross an epoch boundary.

The next independently established MM provides a new synchronization point.

⸻

8. IR through stops

The previous NAVI_COHERENCE behavior treated non-TRACKING IR around stops as invalid distance. That is contrary to the clarified model.

Normal sequence:

movement → deceleration → zero movement → dwell → restart → movement

is one continuous IR measurement interval if the measuring system remained functional.

During the dwell, distance increment is simply zero.

Do not throw away IR because the locomotive stopped.

⸻

9. IR failure and recovery

If IR itself becomes unavailable:

* notify;
* NAVI continues using its non-IR resources where permitted;
* do not pretend the lost IR distance is known.

When IR recovers:

* movement/distance/speed measurement resumes immediately in a new IR Epoch;
* MM-referenced distance remains unavailable until a new MM synchronization is established.

Do not require an arbitrary ten-magnet “trust earning” period.

The earlier earn-trust model was superseded because it treated IR too much like an autonomous agent rather than a measuring instrument.

⸻

10. IR-defined MM window

When a valid MM distance reference exists, IR supplies the physical distance from the last accepted MM.

Current provisional landmark window remains:

mapped cumulative distance ±15%.

For expected mapped distance D:

0.85D–1.15D

defines the region in which a Hall observation can represent that expected MM.

Important distinction:

Too early

Hall event before the IR-defined window.

It cannot be the expected MM.

Reject the event; keep the expected MM unchanged.

Inside window

Hall observation is physically eligible to be the expected landmark and proceeds through the applicable Hall/polarity rules.

After the window

A Hall event after the window cannot be that expected MM.

If the entire valid window was traversed without a valid observation of the expected MM, the MM itself is missed.

“Missed” is therefore a property of the expected landmark, not a label applied to a late Hall signal.

⸻

11. Hall detection

For the next experimental build, Hall detection is:

absolute departure ≥70 counts from the locked baseline for two consecutive 1-kHz samples.

Detection occurs on the second qualifying sample.

The 70-count value is provisional but now has field support. The previous Toby-specific 38-count value came from older sensor/mount conditions and admits magnetic fringe/rebound transients.

Do not confuse this with a return to closure-based navigation. The Hall event exists at opening/detection.

⸻

12. Locked per-interval Hall baseline

This question has already been resolved and implemented in X22.

The baseline cycle is:

recognize MM N → get clear of N’s field → establish ordinary-track level for N→N+1 → LOCK → detect N+1 against it → repeat

If a new baseline cannot validly be established, retain the previous locked baseline.

Do not reopen the old adaptive-baseline architecture.

NAVI_COHERENCE’s successful AUTO operation supplied field evidence for this X22 architecture.

⸻

13. Hall-only timing when IR distance is unavailable

The historical 500-ms guard had an older reference frame involving closure. Detection became the new anchor without re-deriving the number.

Current provisional decision 0093 is:

650 ms detection-to-detection.

It is a Hall-only fallback when usable IR/MM-referenced distance is unavailable.

Evidence from the current Toby architecture showed:

* known false events clustered at ~402 ms;
* closest genuine next-MM event ~894 ms;
* 650 ms sits approximately midway between those populations.

A proposed PWM-dependent 700→1000-ms ramp added complexity without demonstrated benefit in the current evidence and was rejected for now.

If the 650-ms guard occasionally suppresses a genuine MM, NAVI is designed to tolerate a missed magnet.

⸻

14. Wrong polarity

If a Hall event:

* passes the Hall detector;
* lies inside the valid IR MM-distance window;
* but has the wrong polarity,

the polarity observation is retained as contradictory evidence.

One wrong polarity does not relocate NAVI.

Repeated contradictions trigger investigation of physically possible proximal alternatives using route history, IR progression, PWM/motion evidence and the MM map.

⸻

15. Failure-mode reasoning belongs in NAVI

IR reports measurement. NAVI knows locomotive state.

Examples:

Intended stop + IR 0
→ coherent.

Movement commanded + IR 0 + no Hall progression
→ locomotive is not progressing; established shutdown/notification rule applies.

Healthy IR movement + Hall progression
→ coherent.

Healthy IR 0 + successive Hall landmarks
→ presently unexplained physical contradiction. Record/notify; do not make IR invent a diagnosis. NAVI can operate without IR input.

Healthy IR movement but expected Hall progression absent
→ automatic landmark navigation is unavailable. During AUTO, use normal End Auto Operations, which stops the locomotive; manual remains available.

⸻

16. Implementation strategy

Do not rewrite NAVI all at once.

Preserve the parts of NAVI_COHERENCE 0.5 that worked well:

* X22 locked Hall baseline;
* Hall task/event architecture;
* RouteMap;
* station machine;
* AUTO integration;
* IR transport;
* telemetry;
* mapped-distance infrastructure;
* existing missed/multi-step recovery infrastructure where compatible.

Implement the clarified concepts in bounded layers.

Recommended order:

1. Finish and test IR health/readiness/IR Epoch/MM-reference classes independently.
2. Wire those into the existing IR transport and telemetry without changing NAVI decisions.
3. Verify zero movement, stop/dwell continuity, genuine failure, recovery and MM re-synchronization.
4. Apply the approved 70-count Hall detector.
5. Apply the approved provisional 650-ms detection-to-detection Hall-only fallback.
6. Implement exact early / in-window / missed ±15% behavior.
7. Only after those pieces are stable, replace the existing whole-route 10/10 sequence correction with the decisional-inertia / physically-possible / ±10 outer bound / rolling ~3 m history correction model.

Do not let implementation invent new policy.

Where the approved design does not specify behavior, stop and report the unresolved choice rather than filling it in.

⸻

Governing engineering principle

The twenty-questions exercise exposed the principle we want CODEX to preserve:

Measurements report what they physically measure. NAVI owns interpretation.

Hall reports a magnetic observation.

IR reports wheel travel.

PWM reports commanded propulsion state.

The MM map reports surveyed railway geometry and polarity.

NAVI combines those facts with its existing world view and changes that world view only when the physically possible evidence justifies doing so.

That should give CODEX both the decisions and, importantly, the reasoning architecture behind them.
