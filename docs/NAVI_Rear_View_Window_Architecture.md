# NAVI_Rear_View_Window Architecture
**NGR / NAVI_RVM — architectural decision consolidation**  
**17 October 2026 · Status: proposed governing design for research and validation; NOT deployed firmware**

## Purpose and mission
NAVI's normal operational question is **“Have we entered the next tile so that its prescribed orders can execute?”** The physical railroad has a known topology, direction and ordered mile-marker (MM) sequence. Following operator declaration, normal progression is expected. NAVI recognizes the **continuous MM–baseline (BL)–MM magnetic landscape**, rather than requiring every individual Hall opening, polarity or IR distance to pass an isolated test. The future sequence is known; the past is remembered; the present is interpreted.

This document consolidates operator decisions from the October 16–17 RVM design dialogue. Superseded answers are not silently retained as requirements. Superscript-style references [^Dnn] identify **alternatives and reasons** in the footnotes. The decisions describe intended behavior, not demonstrated algorithmic performance. No operational firmware change is authorized.

## 1. Operating model
1. **Single expected progression.** After an operator-declared startup position and direction, NAVI knows the next MM and associated tile orders. Physical outcomes under a movement command are **progression (1)** or **no progression (0)**. Neither individual anomalies nor missing observations create a new geographic identity. A wrong starting declaration is a startup-acquisition concern, not a recurring explanation after long coherent operation.[^D01]
2. **Map plus accumulated pattern are primary.** NAVI maintains a persistent alignment between the learned magnetic landscape and the unfolding continuous Hall trace. Granular Hall features, IR, timestamps and PWM help interpret this pattern; none alone can veto a coherent overall progression. Local observations remain available in their original form.[^D02]
3. **MM–BL–MM is the unit of pattern progression.** The pattern is not reconstructed afresh around each magnet. Recognition is persistent across the journey; the next recognizable ensemble in the expected sequence ordinarily advances the MM count even if its shape is atypical. A discrepancy at MM061 is recorded, but is **not** used as the origin for measuring MM062. Subsequent data are evaluated against the trusted ongoing alignment from MM050–060. The anomaly does not reset the pattern.[^D03]
4. **Rearview interpretation.** The full smoothed waveform is compared to the learned landscape. Magnetic ensembles are fuzzy geographic regions, potentially including multiple lobes, undershoots, polarity changes and apparent flat baseline impostors. A transient flat lobe is not independently a geographic BL. No precise cellular-like boundaries need be assigned before the whole pattern can be recognized. **MM–BL–MM** provides retrospective context.[^D04]
5. **Tile advancement and timing.** The ordinary tile transition is recognized on completion of the ensemble and return to the recognizable baseline/pattern, not on first Hall opening. At the same time, a recognizable atypical next bump can be counted in the expected sequence without becoming a trusted *measurement anchor*. The apparent tension between a completed MM–BL recognition and later MM–BL–MM retrospective corroboration is a **timing/causality validation question**, not permission to invent provisional controller transitions. One geographic advancement per physical ensemble; never one per lobe.[^D05]
6. **Station control remains prescribed.** Preserve existing station landmarks, MM targets and tile/PWM stop routines during RVM development. Station-control adjustments, if needed after field measurement, must be independently configurable for each station in **CW and CCW** (landmark, approach, stopping distance and PWM ramp). No continuous closed-loop speed control is reintroduced. No mid-run direction reversal behavior is in scope.[^D06]

## 2. Signal representation and recognition
7. **Continuous Hall landscape, not presegmented events.** Preserve native Hall ADC samples and provenance. Use an empirically selected low-pass / multiscale “fuzzy brush” to suppress small variations while retaining broad magnetic landscape structure. Compare continuous traces directly; do not require individual MM opening decisions or polarity DNA to qualify the match.[^D07]
8. **Persistent whole-landscape alignment.** Maintain ongoing alignment against the expected geographic portion of the reference, not a fixed 5- or 10-magnet decision reset, and not a short per-magnet window enlarged only after trouble. Constrained DTW is a *candidate*, not a chosen validated algorithm. Alignment constraints, spatial search width, computational bounds and the effect of PWM history must be derived and tested. Ensemble width is **not** automatically half the nominal magnet spacing.[^D08]
9. **Fuzzy statistical reference.** Begin with existing successful CW traversals. Smooth and geographically align validated complete laps, then build a statistical **corridor** of ordinary amplitude, shape, baseline, temporal and apparent spatial variation—not one perfect waveform. Patio track movement and thermal effects make small differences expected. Use a rolling/recency-weighted history, initially **about 20 validated laps** as a research candidate; exact window, weighting and coverage are empirical choices.[^D09]
10. **Match judgment.** Use empirical **whole-pattern similarity** to the learned corridor, not isolated threshold acceptance and not an absolute ADC perfection criterion. The corridor is not a hard veto: an atypical bump can coexist with an overall recognizable landscape. Preserve a relative-alignment comparison as an alternative if whole-pattern statistical similarity fails validation.[^D10]
11. **Persistence of disagreement.** An isolated mismatched tooth in the zipper does not reset alignment. Continue looking for MM062, then MM063, against the established pattern if MM061 is off. The proposed serious incoherence signal is **about five successive expected magnetic features whose *collective pattern* no longer matches**—not five individual pass/fail gates; replay must validate the number and decision timing. Distinguish this from a separate five-*round* maintenance rule.[^D11]
12. **Flat-lobe research.** Measure the longest flat baseline-impostor region *inside* a single confirmed ensemble versus the shortest genuine inter-ensemble BL, using original October 15 data, smoothing-scale comparisons, and credible PWM/IR displacement ranges. Do not use Phase 3 classifications as independent physical truth or convert a measured distribution into a new hard gate without review.[^D12]

## 3. Evidence, progression and faults
13. **PWM is mandatory context.** Applied PWM, elapsed time, ramp history and observed historical speed at relevant geographic locations provide the normal movement expectation. PWM is not guaranteed movement: a stall can occur at high PWM, and downhill travel can exceed a historical nominal speed. PWM has no guaranteed positive minimum distance; its upper physical bound is not established solely by old observations. It is predictive and protective, not a geographic veto.[^D13]
14. **IR supports; it does not veto.** IR may be systematically short or otherwise inaccurate. It must not defeat a coherent Hall/map pattern. Historical IR errors may be stored for sensor analysis, **not written into the physical geographic map**. Fresh IR observations remain eligible immediately when credible.[^D14]
15. **No inherited sensor guilt.** “Quick to condemn; quick to forgive.” A bad Hall observation cannot prohibit using the next Hall observation; the same applies to IR. Preserve anomalies for analysis, but do not let a rejected observation contaminate the next measurement reference, permanent sensor credibility, or the geographic anchor. Sensor health and geographic confidence are distinct.[^D15]
16. **Missing evidence versus lost pattern.** Recording gaps, absent individual MM signatures and a single misplaced tooth are not automatic STOPs. The expected geographic sequence persists and later evidence can confirm a missed observation. NAVI should continue when the preponderance supports progression and station instructions can still be executed safely. Do not synthesize Hall encounters. If the accumulated pattern and other available evidence cannot support a defensible tile, **LOSS OF PATTERN → controlled STOP**; operator redeclaration is required.[^D16]
17. **Movement failure.** When PWM commands travel but magnetic progression ceases and other evidence supports a physical stall/obstruction, **LOSS OF PROGRESSION → E-STOP**, inspection and operator geographic redeclaration. Do not confuse a stationary Hall trace with an invalid baseline or a new magnet. Independent safety protections remain active even during watchful waiting.[^D17]
18. **STOP/GO memory.** Ordinary station STOP, PWM 0, or an hour stopped inside a magnet does **not** reset baseline, position, pattern history, current MM or ensemble. PWM-0 Hall observations are recorded but cannot establish geographic advancement, a new MM or a new baseline for navigation. On RESUME continue the previously developing ensemble, require genuine intervening BL before counting a new one, and avoid duplicate tile advancement. This presumes a powered pause without physical relocation or reboot.[^D18]

## 4. Learning, inspection and distribution
19. **Start with the record; learn only from completed laps.** Operator declares starting position and direction and is trusted immediately; startup SOP places the Hall sensor clear of a magnet. First MM and then MM–BL–MM observations begin matching the loaded reference. The current lap is used for navigation but **never modifies the learned model until the entire lap is completed and validated**. An E-STOP disqualifies the lap from learning; preserve every raw recording regardless.[^D19]
20. **Normal change is absorbed; large change is investigated.** Minor validated waveform/spacing variation contributes to the fuzzy corridor. A major local change (examples: apparently absent ensemble or >50% reduction in magnetic swing—**provisional magnitude requiring validation**) is tracked without silently becoming normal. If the **same major discrepancy persists at the same geographic location for five consecutive rounds**, STOP, notify the operator, require inspection, adjust/repair only if necessary, then operator RESTART. The next complete round is recorded and evaluated; restart acknowledges inspection, not necessarily repair or automatic model replacement. This five-round protocol is **not** the five-feature incoherence rule.[^D20]
21. **Hybrid model storage.** Raspberry Pi archives raw runs, validates completed laps and builds versioned statistical references; each locomotive ESP32 carries a verified local navigational copy. Loss of Pi, Wi-Fi or MQTT must not interrupt navigation. The prior reference remains available for rollback. Trackside ESP/Hall geographic anchors are a future **unproven** enhancement and cannot be assumed by RVM.[^D21]
22. **Live reference update.** A fully validated model may transfer during operation. ESP32 checks integrity, compatibility and version, then activates it at a completed MM–BL–MM progression without resetting geographic position/history. Do not require an additional vague “old/new model consistency” veto; **test** that live activation preserves alignment. Retain rollback and original training provenance.[^D22]

## 5. Interfaces and invariants
- **Observer output:** raw and smoothed Hall, time/serial/gaps, applied PWM, IR quality, developing and completed landscape evidence; do not rewrite original samples.
- **RVM output:** current tile, expected next tile, persistent pattern alignment/coherence, local discrepancy and its persistence, sensor credibility, progression/no-progression, reasoned stop request. Keep diagnostic alternatives separate from controller position.
- **Controller:** execute preexisting tile instructions upon a single recognized advancement; do not create multiple advancements from one magnetic ensemble; independent E-STOP remains authoritative.
- **Reference learning:** only complete validated laps; E-STOP disqualification; versioned immutable inputs and model provenance; no mid-lap training feedback.
- **Stop semantics:** ordinary STOP/GO preserves in-RAM state; protective stop after loss of pattern/progression requires inspection/redeclaration as specified.
- **No feedback spiral:** a discrepant MM cannot reset the origin for judging the next MM. The trusted geographic sequence and independent recorded observations remain available.
- **Direction:** CW/CCW configurations may be separate; no reversal while running is part of RVM.

## 6. Open engineering work — not silently decided
1. Derive and validate the actual smoothing, alignment and statistical similarity mathematics from native repeated laps; benchmark ESP32 timing/memory and telemetry loss. DTW remains one candidate.
2. Resolve the causal timing of a reliable MM–BL completion match versus later MM–BL–MM corroboration and station instruction timing. Do not change existing station landmarks before field calibration.
3. Establish defensible behavior during extended total Hall loss or persistent disagreement when a stop order is imminent; preserve the chosen “continue through isolated misses” philosophy while independently validating safety.
4. Measure baseline-impostor flat lobes versus genuine BL with source uncertainty; determine whether extent helps pattern recognition.
5. Validate the five-feature coherence proposal, five-round maintenance rule, >50% swing example, ~20-lap learning window and model update behavior. None are demonstrated reliability limits.
6. Validate startup misdeclaration detection, stalls (including Grillers), PWM-zero dwell for 60 minutes on a magnet, sensor dropouts and recovery, atypical MM061 followed by correct MM062, and one-to-one tile progression.
7. Validate reference model lineage and complete-lap gating; verify model transfer cannot change position or block offline operation.
8. Verify all earlier governing requirements and identify any formal decision records superseded by this **proposed** RVM design before firmware work.

## 7. Architectural decision footnotes — alternatives retained
[^D01]: Alternative: repeatedly search 171 positions or maintain competing geographic identities. Rejected for normal established navigation because the operator declaration, direction and known sequence give one expected successor; startup misdeclaration remains possible.
[^D02]: Alternative: allow an individual IR interval, Hall polarity or PWM distance gate to determine geographic truth. Rejected after repeated false geographic crises from imperfect measurements.
[^D03]: Alternative: reject/count-hold on any atypical MM, or re-anchor distance at MM061. Rejected because it propagates a local outlier into MM062 and later.
[^D04]: Alternative: hard waveform closure/individual-lobe segmentation as prerequisite to recognizing landscape. Rejected because flat lobes and varying ensembles defeat local boundary decisions.
[^D05]: Alternative: first-opening or provisional tile advancement. Deferred because operator selected retrospective completion; exact controller timing remains a validation issue.
[^D06]: Alternative: recalibrate stops and restore continuous speed feedback now. Rejected as unnecessary coupling before basic RVM operation; retain per-station/direction future adjustment.
[^D07]: Alternative: ensemble descriptors and polarity sequence as mandatory first-stage recognition. Rejected because it returns to narrow, fallible event classification.
[^D08]: Alternatives: short adaptive per-magnet context, fixed five/ten-event window, or unconstrained DTW. Rejected as loss of persistent context or risk of implausible warping. Bounded incremental computation is still needed.
[^D09]: Alternatives: one “golden” lap, immutable lifetime average, or unbounded equally weighted history. Rejected as brittle, stale or increasingly insensitive to recent legitimate variation.
[^D10]: Alternative: choose among competing geographic alignments by relative likelihood. Retained as fallback research, not routine operational competing positions.
[^D11]: Alternative: stop on first anomaly or five independent threshold failures. Rejected because whole-pattern persistence, not isolated measurements, is the criterion.
[^D12]: Alternative: impose baseline duration by definition. Rejected because the longest flat lobe and shortest true BL can be measured and may overlap.
[^D13]: Alternatives: PWM as proof of displacement, zero predictive value, or an unqualified fixed speed ceiling. Rejected due to stalls, useful historical PWM response, and grade effects respectively.
[^D14]: Alternatives: IR as distance veto, always-undermeasuring lower bound, or map rewrite from IR bias. Rejected because IR can be wrong in either direction and the map describes geography.
[^D15]: Alternative: sticky strikes, permanent loss of sensor credibility or disqualification of the next observation. Rejected for self-reinforcing errors.
[^D16]: Alternative: automatic STOP on every missing Hall encounter or recording gap. Rejected because missing observation is not proof of missing geography. No unrestricted blind travel is authorized.
[^D17]: Alternative: treat absent Hall progress as conclusive stall without other evidence. Rejected because Hall sensor failure can mimic stationary signal.
[^D18]: Alternatives: reset baseline at PWM 0, time out the ensemble, or count restart lobes as new MM. Rejected because stopped time is not movement and geography persists.
[^D19]: Alternative: rebuild the model from each session or train on partial laps. Rejected because early noisy observations must not modify their own reference.
[^D20]: Alternatives: automatically learn every persistent large change, require operator acceptance for every small change, or require a repair on every inspection. Rejected as unsafe model drift, burdensome, and unnecessary intervention respectively.
[^D21]: Alternatives: Pi-dependent real-time navigation or ESP32-only archival learning. Rejected due to connectivity dependence or limited storage/analysis capacity.
[^D22]: Alternatives: activate only at next session, or add a second undefined model-consistency gate. Rejected because it forces stale references or unnecessary complexity; retain old/new comparison as a research fallback.
