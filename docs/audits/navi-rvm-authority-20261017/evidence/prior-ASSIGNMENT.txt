1. Governing objective

NAVI’s primary responsibility is reliable geographic navigation.

The DART model identifies one qualifying Hall observation at each expected geographic target.

The IR system measures movement. When IR measurements are geographically credible, DART uses distance to compare candidate magnetic observations.

When IR is unavailable or unreliable, DART uses the previous lap’s recorded arrival times, when available.

The governing sequence remains:

Observe → Qualify → Compare → Select → Advance.

Preserve the established principles:

* One selected observation per geographic target.
* No manufactured Hall events.
* No automatic geographic advancement without a qualifying Hall observation.
* No historical ±15% distance gate.
* No 650 ms fallback rule.
* No morphology-based exclusion of otherwise qualifying Hall observations.
* No arbitrary global relocation or search of all 171 magnets.

Do not redesign geographic DART.

5. IR credibility — preserve the approved rules

Retain the provisional criteria:

Absolute interval discrepancy	Interpretation
≤10 mm	Consistent
>10 mm but <5 IR pulses	Inconsistent but possible
≥5 IR pulses	Withdraw IR geographic authority

At the installed calibration, five pulses correspond to 48.260 mm.

The 10 mm criterion is diagnostic, not a geographic acceptance gate.

The five-pulse threshold withdraws IR authority; it does not invalidate a qualifying Hall observation.

Continue recording IR measurements after authority withdrawal.

Operator redeclaration and reassessment

Implement the approved recovery behavior:

1. A new operator position declaration establishes a fresh geographic context.
2. It permits a new IR credibility assessment.
3. It does not automatically certify IR accuracy.
4. Use a new, appropriately identified Hall opening as the measurement reference.
5. Evaluate the subsequent Hall-to-Hall interval.
6. Restore IR geographic authority only if that interval is possible under the provisional five-pulse rule.

If IR fails again following this operator-initiated reassessment, stop and notify the operator.

Do not enter an unlimited automatic withdrawal/recovery cycle.

Record every transition and its supporting evidence.

7. PWM-zero IR behavior

The operator has clarified that Otto and Toby stop when motor power is removed. Do not introduce speculative coasting behavior into this design.

When applied PWM equals zero, IR observations must not contribute to authorized geographic distance.

Requirements:

* Continue raw optical sampling and diagnostic recording.
* Preserve the underlying IR measurement stream.
* Suppress the geographic use of stationary optical fluctuations.
* Resume geographic use only under the appropriate movement and reference conditions.
* Preserve existing externally imposed movement detection as a separate protection mechanism.

Do not reset cumulative IR counts in a way that corrupts the measurement frame.

The IR transmitter may not know the locomotive’s applied PWM, so enforce geographic authority in NAVI unless the existing architecture provides a better, verified mechanism.

Do not confuse suppression of geographic distance with suppression of diagnostic evidence.

8. Approved IR transmitter experiment — 5/12 completion threshold

Prepare a separately named and identifiable experimental IR transmitter firmware.

The existing production detector uses:

* Opening threshold: 2/3 of the adaptive signal span.
* Completion threshold: 1/3 of the adaptive signal span.

For the experimental transmitter:

* Preserve the opening threshold at 2/3.
* Change the completion threshold to 5/12.
* Preserve all other detector behavior unless a change is strictly required for the experiment.

Do not change distance calibration, wheel pitch, pulses per revolution, or geographic map.

The 5/12 modification permits shallower optical troughs to complete a cycle.

Earlier replay recovered the four disputed October 14 troughs, but did not establish that the change improves physical counting accuracy across operating conditions.

This is an authorized field experiment, not a validated detector replacement.

Experimental controls

Record the detector version and completion fraction at startup.

Preserve the original transmitter firmware for rollback.

Where practical, retain the ability to compare the unchanged 1/3 detector against the 5/12 detector on identical recorded ADC input.

Do not manufacture pulses to make the measured distance agree with the map.

Do not deploy the experimental whole-cycle recognizer.

9. Continue Schoolboy’s Ear development separately

The longer-term objective remains reliable recognition of complete optical cycles from the physical waveform.

The October 14 investigations established:

* Strong waveform repetition near 80–91 ms.
* Recognizable optical excursions rejected by the current detector.
* Useful causal waveform development within approximately 14–17 ms.
* Limitations in the first morphology and timing-based recognizers.

Continue collecting raw optical ADC evidence during EWO_0_6 operation.

The 5/12 experiment may improve the existing detector, but it is not the final answer to waveform recognition.

Do not delay EWO_0_6 waiting for a complete new optical-recognition architecture.
