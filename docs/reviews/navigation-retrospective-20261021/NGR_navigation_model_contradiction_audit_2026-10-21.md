# NGR navigation model contradiction audit

**Purpose:** identify incompatible assumptions across the historical models. A contradiction here means that two models give different authority to the same evidence or prescribe incompatible behavior in the same physical state. It is not merely different terminology.

## Resolved contradictions

| Question | Earlier position | Later / competing position | Evidence and disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Can one unexpected Hall event relocate or stop navigation?** | DNA/NAVI_ONE’s operational forms could treat mismatch as a direct strike; RVM navigator treats wrong-polarity accepted ensemble as immediate CONTRADICTION. | QUORUM and NAVI_SIMPLIFIED treat a surprising event as the opening of an evaluation among alternatives. | Field record strongly rejects an immediate operational strike: NAVI_ONE logged 115 wrong-magnet stops in 71 sessions. Preserve the *diagnostic* alarm, not the single-event navigation verdict. RVM’s otherwise strong field result does not validate its no-recovery boundary. |
| **Are apparent missed-magnet bursts evidence that the odometer is behind?** | QUORUM’s original asymmetric fence `{−1,0,+1,+2,+3,+4}` assumed multiple misses were common and multiple phantoms were not. | Label-slip analysis found that many later “misses” were cascades after the count had already slipped; phantom/rejection mechanisms could create the error that the model then read as multiple new misses. | The original statistical premise is contradicted. The holdout replay supported symmetric `{-3…+4}` as an unapproved candidate. Do not use observed post-slip rejection runs as raw physical-miss statistics. |
| **Is waveform shape allowed to veto a Hall magnet?** | NAVI_ONE used residual/shape refusal as navigation authority. | X13 discussion, SIMPLIFIED, and later decision work make morphology diagnostic unless independently justified as a physical suppression. | Repeated false stops near the ordinary residual boundary refute routine shape veto. F09A shows shape retained useful diagnostic signal under a known acquisition latch. Preserve the data and high-severity alarm; do not give borderline morphology automatic route authority. |
| **Does a detector reopening/closure prove another mapped magnet?** | Several Hall lines coupled rearm/closure to later navigation eligibility. | NAVI_SIMPLIFIED explicitly separates electrical rearm, Hall closure, and physical same-magnet protection. | The SIMPLIFIED field failure demonstrates why the later rule is necessary: repeated readings of one magnet can consume a recovery word. Preserve detector rearm, but never let it establish physical travel by itself. |
| **Is a missed observation a location contradiction?** | Event-driven designs readily interpret an absent/rejected expected event as a count problem. | Later NAVI decision work distinguishes absence of evidence from contradictory polarity evidence; the working world view persists through one imperfect observation. | This distinction is coherent with the cascade diagnosis. A missing event may raise uncertainty; it does not by itself establish a new location. |
| **May timing/PWM prove that an event is impossible?** | QUORUM conservation gate used a PWM velocity prediction as a rejector; its low-PWM hole instead accepted events unconditionally. | SIMPLIFIED requires per-locomotive, direction- and interval-specific conservative physical protection; RVM aligner uses motion as a soft cost, not a veto. | The PWM gate rejected real magnets in runs and created label slips; measured-expectation replacement was also rejected in its tested form. Preserve timing as evidence, but require a conservative physical bound before suppression. |
| **Should an armed station sequence continue through temporary position ambiguity?** | Some NAVI_SIMPLIFIED discussion said preserve an already committed X18 station approach/stop while preventing new arming. | The controlling reconstruction record says the later operator ruling is to skip the stop when position is unsure, pending explicit safe-cancellation semantics. | This is explicitly superseded/unfinished. Do not import either behavior as settled code. The unresolved requirement is a cancellation/hold policy that is safe during every station phase. |
| **May a peer use a reported MM as exact clearance geometry?** | CTO2/early CTO used point-like marker-derived position plus fixed front/rear offsets. | CTO harvest requires a bounded envelope with freshness, direction, uncertainty and consist geometry. | CTO2 rear ends resolve this: exact clearance arithmetic on wrong position is unsafe. Later CTO3/QUORUM did not prove its bounds or braking response adequate, so this remains a hard safety constraint rather than a solved implementation. |

## Important unresolved contradictions

| Question | Competing positions | What the evidence presently says | Safe carry-forward status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What should happen after the first physically possible contradiction?** | NAVI_SIMPLIFIED: preserve ambiguity and gather bounded subsequent evidence. NAVI_ONE/RVM: immediate stop/contradiction. | Field rejects the routine one-event stop; SIMPLIFIED’s only implementation failed for a different reason (same-magnet rereads), so it did not test its intended recovery design. | Preserve ambiguity and evidence collection as the default design direction; require a separately verified limit/controlled-stop policy. |
| **How wide should the alternative-position space be?** | Original QUORUM `−1…+4`; candidate symmetric `−3…+4`; whole-tracing aligner avoids a small discrete fence. | DNA-only and holdout replay favor adding negative candidates. More candidates can create local aliases/ties; the route has a known period-six ambiguity near Arches. | No universal fixed fence is field accepted. Keep the candidate/margin mechanism and map-specific alias analysis; do not assume any single window is safe for every incident. |
| **What is the right Hall baseline model?** | NAVI_ONE dynamic/rolling baseline; frozen baseline; slow/rate-limited or lap-level adaptation; RVM local learned baseline plus continuity. | Thermal/operating shifts are real; a rolling baseline can be contaminated by a stopped magnet. RVM also showed motor-dependent levels and later r5 feedback problems. | Preserve baseline provenance and quiet-state eligibility. Do not allow a baseline estimator to take unqualified authority or feed itself through its own event admission. |
| **What authority should IR have?** | Early EWO often presented IR/distance as a location-support path; later statements range from IR as observer to strong validity-gated motion evidence; aligner uses it as a soft prior. | Toby’s cited Hall-loss episode did not actually add IR inference. Other discussion records show both useful IR rejection of false events and physical arrangements where IR car motion did not equal locomotive motion. | Treat IR as independent evidence with explicit validity/provenance. It must neither silently become sovereign nor be casually discarded solely because Hall disagrees. No field-proven fusion authority rule exists. |
| **Can a locomotive safely stop on a magnet?** | Some detection logic treats a stopped/zero-PWM condition as a veto or reset. Station operation necessarily can dwell on a magnet; RVM continuity was built to preserve held ensembles. | Field history repeatedly shows failures at dwell/restart, while RVM continuity preserved long holds in its demonstrated run. | Preserve stop epoch and last physical anchor; do not count standing rereads as advances. A zero-PWM blanket veto is too crude. |
| **What does “reliable position” mean?** | EWO uses `position_reliable`; CTO needs exact-enough position to protect a peer; QUORUM has normal/evaluating/lost; RVM has tracking/contradiction. | EWO logs show `position_reliable=1` through a degraded Hall episode but give no calibrated remaining error. CTO collisions prove a Boolean is inadequate for traffic. | Publish components: location hypothesis/candidates, uncertainty bounds, last physical anchor, freshness, evidence source, and suitability for station versus peer safety. |
| **Should the recognizer be per-event or whole-path?** | RVM firmware accepts individual ensembles and advances irreversibly; Oct-16 aligner scores the whole trace and may revise the recent past. | RVM has the best live single-locomotive evidence. The aligner is replay-only and Otto-template-specific. | Maintain whole-path alignment as a comparator/research path; do not replace field-proven causal core based on retrospective fit. |
| **Should station/peer logic be integrated with navigation during development?** | RVM AUTO integrated station carpet/bubble early; manual RVM kept navigation observer-only. | The manual/continuity core produced the strongest field evidence. r1–r5.1 integration generated new coupled failures, including Arches contact and later contradictions. | Prove localization separately. Station and peer layers should consume an accepted uncertainty-bearing interface, not shape the recognizer’s evidence path. |

## False contradictions: things that can coexist

| Pair | Why they are compatible if layers are respected |
|---|---|
| DNA route map and RVM waveform ensembles | DNA/map describes *where a passage could belong*; RVM describes *what the Hall landscape looks like*. Neither must by itself make the final position decision. |
| QUORUM alternatives and whole-tracing alignment | Both represent more than one plausible location. QUORUM is a bounded discrete/casual mechanism; aligner is a broader delayed-path estimator. The latter can be a shadow comparator. |
| Geographic station control and temporary navigation ambiguity | A currently established station operation can be represented separately from a fresh position claim, provided the cancellation/hold semantics are explicitly safe. The conflict is not inevitable; the historical rule is unresolved. |
| Field station calibration and a new navigation core | Existing stop ramp/geometry data can be retained as an actuator/station asset, provided it is not used as evidence that a position estimate is correct. |
| Hall and IR | They are complementary observations only if their independent failure modes and physical measurement points are retained. The conflict arises when either becomes an unexamined veto. |

## Most consequential cross-model finding

The models repeatedly disagreed about whether a surprising observation is a **detector error**, a **map/location contradiction**, or a **reason to command motion**. Those are three distinct decisions. The systems that collapsed them generated either false stops (NAVI_ONE), false corrections/cascades (QUORUM), or unsafe consumers of false certainty (CTO2). The most coherent common direction is therefore:

1. record the observation immutably;
2. apply only physically justified same-magnet/impossibility suppression;
3. keep alternatives and uncertainty explicit;
4. let station and CTO layers act only on a position interface that states its usable bounds;
5. retain raw evidence and replay parity so a future claim can be checked.
