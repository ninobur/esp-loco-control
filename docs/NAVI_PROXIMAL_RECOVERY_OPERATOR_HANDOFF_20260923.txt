Here is the concise handoff for CODEX:

NAVI position recovery: do not search the whole 171-MM route

The current behavior that can relocate NAVI to a distant polarity-pattern match is contrary to the decisions reached in the NAVI design review.

1. Decisional inertia

NAVI’s current believed position is its working world view.

One missed magnet, one wrong-polarity observation, or one contradictory datum does not make NAVI globally lost.

NAVI remains where its accumulated route history says it is unless a preponderance of evidence supports a better physically possible position.

A single inaccurate observation does not create ambiguity, much less justify relocation across the railway.

2. Possible before probable

Before comparing polarity-pattern matches, NAVI must eliminate positions that are physically impossible.

A candidate MM is not legitimate merely because its polarity sequence matches recent observations.

The question is first:

Could Toby physically be there, given where NAVI previously knew him to be and the measured travel since then?

Only physically possible positions may enter pattern comparison.

A perfect polarity match at MM163 is irrelevant if Toby’s established history and travel place him near MM029.

3. Never search all 171 positions for ordinary recovery

The whole-route 171-position search is conceptually wrong because it permits mathematically valid but physically impossible solutions.

The absolute outer recovery boundary is:

±10 MM from NAVI’s incumbent believed position.

That is only an outer limit. NAVI should not automatically examine all twenty alternatives.

IR travel, route history, direction and applicable movement/PWM evidence should reduce the candidate set to positions Toby could actually have reached.

4. IR provides a physical bound

When IR is available and MM-referenced distance is valid, IR is the strongest direct measurement of how much wheel travel occurred.

IR should therefore eliminate candidate positions requiring travel that did not occur.

Example:

If polarity could mathematically match offsets +1, +4 and +7, but IR measured travel compatible only with +1, the +4 and +7 hypotheses are not competing alternatives. They are physically impossible and are removed before scoring.

5. Route history remains evidence

The operator’s starting declaration and subsequent accepted MM history ground Toby to a particular region of the Lowline.

That history is not discarded merely because observations become contradictory.

The operator declaration is evidence, not an infinitely precise absolute truth, but recovery remains proximal to the established position.

This is why an incorrect declaration can be corrected without allowing NAVI to teleport to an unrelated matching pattern elsewhere on the railway.

6. Maintain approximately 10 MM / 3,000 mm of rolling physical history

Position recovery uses approximately:

10 MM positions / ~3,000 mm of recent travel history.

This is a physical history, not necessarily ten successful Hall detections.

Within it:

* observed polarity remains evidence;
* a wrong polarity remains evidence;
* a missed MM is UNKNOWN/inconclusive;
* missed magnets do not count for or against a polarity match;
* history is retained after a correction.

After session startup, autonomous repositioning should not occur until approximately this history exists.

7. Compare only the remaining physically possible candidates

After impossible candidates have been eliminated, compare the incumbent and remaining candidates against the available polarity observations in the rolling history.

The denominator is the number of actual polarity observations available, not automatically ten.

For example, if the physical 10-position history contains 8 observed polarities and 2 missed MMs, compare candidates against the 8 observations.

8. Unique better explanation required

The incumbent position retains decisional inertia.

Decision rule:

* incumbent remains best → keep incumbent;
* one physically possible alternative uniquely explains the evidence better → reposition;
* two or more alternatives tie for best → non-decisional; keep incumbent;
* no alternative improves on incumbent → keep incumbent.

No arbitrary additional score margin was approved.

9. A missed magnet is not positional ambiguity

If an expected MM passes without a valid Hall observation, record it as:

MISSED / UNKNOWN

That is absence of polarity evidence at that position.

It does not mean NAVI is suddenly somewhere else.

NAVI continues from its existing world view and evaluates subsequent landmarks.

10. Wrong polarity is evidence, not an immediate relocation

A real Hall observation with wrong polarity remains in history as the polarity actually observed.

One wrong pole does not relocate NAVI.

If contradictory observations accumulate, NAVI then investigates physically possible proximal alternatives using:

* established position/history;
* IR progression;
* direction;
* PWM/motion evidence where useful;
* MM map;
* observed polarity history.

11. Correction does not erase history

If NAVI reassigns position, retain the observations that led to the correction.

The correction changes NAVI’s interpretation of the history. It does not make the physical observations disappear.

If a correction proves wrong, subsequent evidence can cause the process to repeat and correct NAVI again.

12. Governing rule

The recovery process is:

INCUMBENT WORLD VIEW
→ PHYSICALLY POSSIBLE PROXIMAL POSITIONS
→ ELIMINATE WITH IR/TRAVEL/DIRECTION EVIDENCE
→ COMPARE AVAILABLE MM POLARITY HISTORY
→ UNIQUE BETTER EXPLANATION REQUIRED
→ OTHERWISE RETAIN INCUMBENT

Not:

POLARITY DISAGREEMENT
→ SEARCH ALL 171 MMs
→ FIND UNIQUE PATTERN
→ TELEPORT NAVI THERE
