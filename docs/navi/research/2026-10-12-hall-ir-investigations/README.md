# October 12 2026 Hall and IR research archive

This archive preserves the October 12 NAVI investigations so later engineering can inspect the original evidence, reproduce the available analyses, and retain the unsuccessful hypotheses. Most physical observations were recorded on **September 28, 2026**, with Otto. October 12 is the research and review date, not a new railway measurement date.

The evidence reveals repeatable Hall structure and substantial timing limitations in IR odometry. It does **not validate an operational calibrated opening map, morphology classifier, candidate separation threshold, or Type-6 geographic synchronization**. This is research documentation, not a change to NAVI authority, firmware, magnet maps, or calibration.

## How to interpret the evidence

| Evidence class | What belongs in it | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recorded observations | ADC conversions, medians, Hall timestamps, Type-5 counters, packet sequences and original NAVI judgments | What the instruments and software recorded |
| Offline interpretation | Inferred magnet/lobe identities, nominal-distance interpolation, geographic reconstruction of event 488, held-out analyses and recorded-data replay | Findings conditional on the labels, clock model, filtering and one recording |
| Synthetic simulation | Invented 270/295/299 mm impostors and injected Type-6 failure cases | Software or model behavior under stated inputs; not railway incidence |
| Demonstrated software defect | Actual compiler failure and host reproductions using extracted submitted code | A deterministic code defect; not its frequency in operation |

Historical `ADVANCED` and `NON_LANDMARK_HALL` labels are retained as labels generated by NAVI. They are not independent physical identification. A successful replay, a successful build, and a field-accepted navigation improvement are separate outcomes.

## Investigation index

### 1 Hall magnetic field morphology

[Original report](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/REPORT.md) · [machine-readable events and intervals](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/continuous_hall_analysis.json) · [analysis script](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/analyze_continuous_hall.py)

The September 29 MQTT log lacks continuous ADC data; the nearby September 28 synchronized NSR recording supplies 3,377,328 Hall batches, 16,886,640 conversions and 33,264 IR snapshots. All 951 recorded Hall events matched the raw trace and the two-sample opening rule; recorder datagram gaps were zero. The Hall ring-drop counter began at one and stayed one, so this is not a claim that no sample was ever lost before capture.

Nine inferred opposite-lobe sites lay 41.9–128.6 mm after their associated primary openings; the minimum reliable adjacent genuine-opening interval was 257.4 mm. The 128.8 mm observed separation is specific to this recording and the offline lobe definition. Raw shapes were asymmetric, broad or stepped; no Gaussian model was fitted. Detector opening, threshold crossing and physical field boundary are different quantities.

The D−10/D+30 mm hypothesis rejected 104 of 591 reliable genuine intervals, while the existing ±15% diagnostic admitted all 591. This negative result is preserved. The initial 18/18 closest-distance result involved earlier secondary candidates and is narrower than the later causal replay in investigation 5.

For NAVI_EWO_0_4, this supplies measured trace examples and a rejected narrow-window hypothesis. It supplies no universal lobe cutoff or new navigation rule.

### 2 Opening to primary peak

[Original report](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/opening-position/REPORT.md) · [per-magnet readable table](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/opening-position/magnet_opening_variation.md) · [per-passage CSV](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/opening-position/opening_passages.csv) · [script](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/analyze_opening_positions.py)

The analysis retained 585 high-confidence profiles: 529 isolated primary peaks and 56 censored profiles. Every one of the 171 CW magnets had an estimate. CW per-magnet median opening-to-peak distance was 13.13 mm; between-magnet SD was 1.93 mm, compared with median within-magnet SD 2.34 mm among 117 well-repeated magnets. Offsets therefore did not provide a distinctive, stable identity fingerprint.

The physical-center hypothesis failed its proposed geographic check: differences in peak offset explained about 0.08% of interval-error variance and had the wrong-sign fitted slope. Physical centers were not surveyed. This does not prove peaks are displaced from centers; it means the recording did not validate the assumption. Only 26 magnets had a single usable CCW profile, so reverse-direction repeatability remains unresolved.

For 0.4, these are diagnostic profile measurements, not permission to replace Hall opening geography with peak location.

### 3 Opening geographic map and tape spacing

[Original map report](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/opening-map/REPORT.md) · [calibrated interval CSV](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/opening-map/calibrated_opening_intervals.csv) · [held-out trials](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/opening-map/held_out_candidate_trials.csv) · [script](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/analyze_ir_odometry_calibration.py)

There were 386 high-confidence observations in 175 directed interval groups; 145 groups repeated. All 356 leave-one-passage-out trials were CW. Capture-aligned interpolation improved RMSE from 9.88 to 6.78 mm using a learned opening map. The actual recorded integer-pulse measurement went the other way: 12.82 mm RMSE with physical spacing versus 13.24 mm with calibration. Both maps selected the genuine opening in 356/356 held-out trials; only eight trials contained qualifying intervening false candidates, with a minimum winning margin of 48.26 mm.

The earlier peak report gives 9.98→6.94 mm for its own interval calculation. These are separate filtering/measurement analyses, not interchangeable estimates. The map report's distinction between runtime integer distance and offline interpolation governs claims about operational benefit.

The capture-era tape-spacing array totals 52,150 mm. Three intervals were subsequently changed in the compared map. Tape spacing is an estimated physical reference, not contemporaneous surveyed opening coordinates. The physical railroad, magnets, sensor mounting and wheel behavior may have changed since September 28. No archived map is installed or promoted by this work.

### 4 IR accuracy and synchronization limitations

[Odometry findings](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/opening-map/REPORT.md) · [timing and quantization plot](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/opening-map/plots/ir_timing_and_quantization.png) · [MM115 to 116 diagnostic](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/opening-map/mm115_116_diagnostic.csv)

Every snapshot used nominal pitch 9.652 mm, calibration ID 0 and `distanceValidated=0`; nominal distance arithmetic exactly matched cumulative pulse counts. This verifies configuration, not actual millimeters traveled. There were 435 Type-5 sequence gaps totaling 484 missing report positions, with no counter regression. Later cumulative reports recover counter values; missing reports do not by themselves prove missing optical pulses.

The ideal independent-endpoint quantization SD is 3.94 mm, but recorded integer distance minus capture-aligned interpolation had SD 9.93 mm. Linear interpolation does not create independent sub-pulse measurements. Global minimum-offset alignment also cannot separate clock drift from one-way transport latency. MM115→116 retained substantial variation after interpolation, so timing and quantization do not explain every discrepancy.

For 0.4, pulse-counter consistency, measurement freshness, and complete physical wheel-mark counting must remain distinct claims. The recording cannot prove the physical no-missed-pulses premise or absolute calibration.

### 5 Closest candidate selection and morphology assistance

[Original report](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/morphology-candidate/REPORT.md) · [causal replay](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/morphology-candidate/candidate_replay.csv) · [synthetic cases](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/morphology-candidate/synthetic_impostor_cases.csv) · [script](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/analyze_morphology_candidates.py)

The causal closest-distance model retains the candidate with minimum absolute error from expected D. An early candidate at x must wait to the symmetric boundary `D + abs(D − x)` before later candidates cannot improve on it. That finality delay is part of the proposed model, not an installed NAVI rule.

Treating every opening as independent selected correctly in 385/386 replays; a same-field reopening one pulse after the genuine opening won the failed case. Requiring baseline return reduced success to 384/386 by suppressing genuine subsequent openings. Post-hoc separation of two to five pulses achieved 386/386; six or more began suppressing genuine observations. The threshold was fitted to these same data and has no independent validation. The historical first-qualified comparator was already correct in 386/386, so no measured identification improvement was demonstrated.

The held-out rise-plus-peak classifier had AUC 0.938, but rejected 56/585 provisional genuine openings and accepted 15/84 secondary labels. It is unsuitable as a veto. Post-hoc opening-rise reranking repaired one actual near-tie; that single case cannot validate a combined classifier. Synthetic expected-polarity impostors at 270, 295 and 299 mm were invented test inputs. The 295/300 and 299/300 pairs tie at the recorded pulse resolution; no such physical pairs were observed.

For 0.4, the preserved result is a set of hypotheses and failure cases, including the cost of waiting for symmetric finality. Neither morphology nor the successful in-sample separation band acquires navigation authority.

### 6 Raw events 254 through 257

[Raw waveform report](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/waveform-254-257/REPORT.md) · [254 and 255 plot](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/waveform-254-257/events_254_255_raw_adc.png) · [256 and 257 plot](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/waveform-254-257/events_256_257_raw_adc.png) · [metrics](originals/hall-field-analysis-20260929/waveform-254-257/waveform_metrics.csv)

For the requested MM125→126 and MM126→127 event pairs, measured nominal opening displacement was 155.397 and 106.750 mm. All four references were 2007. Median ADC stayed +53 to +74 and +57 to +74 counts above reference, respectively: no return to the ±25 band. The first interval had one isolated raw conversion in the band; the second had none. Crossing below the +70 opening threshold was therefore distinct from baseline return.

Events 254 and 256 were recorded as `NON_LANDMARK_HALL`; 255 and 257 were `ADVANCED`. These pairs are not two confirmed map landmarks separated by the tape spacing. The later raw report explicitly says the measurements **do not establish physical magnetic-field overlap**. Preserve that qualification when reading the earlier morphology report's stronger wording that “fields overlap.”

### 7 Reporting frequency and Hall timing

[Original review](originals/ir-reporting-frequency-review-20261012/REPORT.md) · [metrics](originals/ir-reporting-frequency-review-20261012/sept28_ir_age_metrics.json) · [script](originals/ir-reporting-frequency-review-20261012/measure_sept28_ir_age.py)

“Current” in this report means the inspected October 12 snapshot at `4ad1df2`, not any future checkout or installed transmitter. Type 5 was scheduled every 100 ms; Type 6 carried completed-pulse events; Type 7 supplied 1 Hz transport status. NAVI 0.3 used Type-5 receiver-arrival boundaries for Hall geography and Type-6 timing only for credibility. September 28 instead used minimum-offset capture alignment and nearest-snapshot association.

Applying the newer last-arrival-before-Hall rule offline to the 806 mapped primary openings gave receiver-arrival age median 48.419 ms, p95 95.213 ms and maximum 174.881 ms. This is not measured optical-capture age or end-to-end latency. The earlier selected-snapshot alignment figures answer a different timing question.

Legacy 50 ms unicast delivery supported a faster-periodic experiment, but packet size, broadcast destination and mixed traffic differed. The October 6 Type-6 run's 32.33% receiver-observed discontinuity did not localize the loss. A proposed Type-6 Hall coordinate still needs clock association, delivery validation and an explicit decision-delay treatment; interpolation remains inferred distance. No 20 or 10 ms reliability claim is established.

### 8 Complete circuit pulse counts

[Original report](originals/ir-pulse-count-circuits-20260928/REPORT.md) · [circuit CSV](originals/ir-pulse-count-circuits-20260928/circuit_counts.csv) · [endpoint CSV](originals/ir-pulse-count-circuits-20260928/endpoint_counts.csv) · [recovered original commands](provenance/pulse-count-session-commands.json)

Three geographically identified CW circuits used event boundaries 315→488→665→836 and selected cumulative counts 10,625→15,967→21,352→26,703. Exact recorded counter differences are **5,342, 5,385 and 5,351 pulses**, totaling **16,078**; mean 5,359.33, sample SD 22.68 and range 43 pulses. These were counted before converting distance.

Event 488 is an offline reconstructed MM000 opening rejected online; event 836 was accepted with a polarity discrepancy. Only 315 and 665 satisfy the narrower confirmed-endpoint rule, enclosing two circuits and 10,727 pulses. Aligned snapshot brackets give possible opening-to-opening ranges 5,336–5,341, 5,386–5,390 and 5,348–5,352. These brackets depend on the archived clock-association model and do not supply exact individual pulse times.

The 52,150 mm map implies 9.7307 map-mm/pulse over all three circuits. It is a map comparison, not a new calibrated constant. Each circuit included four scheduled station stops with no reversal or e-stop. Initial recovery and the incomplete terminal CW/CCW portions were excluded. No complete CCW circuit exists in this capture.
