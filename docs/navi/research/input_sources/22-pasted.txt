Stopping Overlay

Yes. That is the larger concept.

What we are currently calling the station STOP sequence is really the first implementation of a reusable stopping overlay.

Conceptually, whenever automatic operation requires NAVI to stop, the dispatcher can impose a stopping overlay at the appropriate location. That overlay contains the geographic instructions necessary to execute the stop:

approach/deceleration tile(s) → terminus tile → physical stop → dwell/hold as required → restart/release

The location and reason for the stop can vary:

* station service;
* spacing behind another consist;
* holding point;
* dispatcher-commanded stop;
* eventually other automatic operating situations.

The stopping mechanism remains fundamentally the same. What changes is where the overlay is placed, perhaps its approach geometry, and what condition governs dwell/release.

That also gives “terminus tile” a useful general meaning: it isn’t a station concept. It is the tile containing the intended terminus of an automatic stopping maneuver.

And our narrow state-transfer rule then makes architectural sense beyond stations:

Within a stopping overlay, the terminus tile may accept the instantaneous physical braking state produced by the preceding stopping-overlay tile so that one continuous physical deceleration can cross the tile boundary.

That is much cleaner than building “station stopping” as a special procedural system. It is a general NAVI capability that station service happens to use first.
