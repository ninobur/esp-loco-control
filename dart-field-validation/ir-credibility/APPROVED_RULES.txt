CODEX — NAVI EWO _0_4: IR Credibility Decision Rules

Date: October 14, 2026
Status: Approved provisional operating criteria, pending field validation.

We have resolved the outstanding questions concerning IR inconsistency, withdrawal of geographic authority, and restoration.

These decisions are intended for implementation in the working NAVI EWO _0_4 sketch.

1. Governing principle

NAVI trusts IR distance when it is consistent with established geographic knowledge.

IR is a measurement instrument, not an independent geographic authority.

NAVI determines geographic identity using qualified Hall observations and the dart-model comparison.

When IR measurements contradict credible geographic evidence, NAVI must recognize the contradiction rather than repeatedly reject genuine magnets.

The historical ±15% distance gate and 650 ms fallback are eliminated entirely.

2. Provisional IR discrepancy criteria

The current evidence is imperfect. We have therefore adopted temporary numerical criteria to permit field testing.

Measured discrepancy	Classification	Action
≤10 mm	Consistent	Continue normal operation
>10 mm but <5 IR pulses	Inconsistent but possible	Record discrepancy; retain IR authority
≥5 IR pulses	Impossible under the provisional operating rule	Withdraw IR geographic authority immediately

At the installed nominal calibration:

5 pulses × 9.652 mm = 48.260 mm.

Exactly five pulses of discrepancy triggers withdrawal.

These limits apply to the absolute difference between the measured Hall-to-Hall interval and the expected geographic interval.

The 10 mm criterion is for IR credibility assessment only. It must not become a geographic candidate-acceptance gate.

Likewise, the five-pulse criterion must not cause an otherwise qualifying Hall observation to be discarded.

3. Immediate withdrawal

One interval exhibiting a discrepancy of five or more IR pulses is sufficient to withdraw IR geographic authority.

Do not require repeated failures.

When withdrawal occurs:

1. Preserve the qualifying Hall observation and geographic interpretation.
2. Record the measured and expected distances.
3. Record the discrepancy in millimeters and pulse equivalents.
4. Mark IR as geographically unreliable.
5. Continue collecting IR measurements.
6. Switch geographic candidate comparison to the established historical timing dart model.
7. Report the withdrawal and its supporting evidence.

IR transport health and IR geographic credibility remain separate.

A coherent stream of IR packets does not override evidence that its distance measurements are geographically impossible.

4. Restoration of IR authority

Withdrawal does not mean that IR measurements cease.

The next independently identified 70×2 Hall opening establishes a fresh measurement reference.

Measure the interval from that opening to the following identified Hall opening.

If that new interval produces a possible distance measurement—meaning its discrepancy is less than five IR pulses—IR geographic authority may be restored.

The new interval must have applicable IR measurements and a defensible geographic identification. Do not use IR’s disputed measurement as the sole proof of the identity needed to restore IR authority.

Restoration must not retroactively validate the interval that caused withdrawal.

Record the restoration event and its supporting measurement.

A discrepancy greater than 10 mm but below five pulses remains inconsistent, but possible under the provisional rule.

5. Geographic navigation during IR withdrawal

The dart model remains NAVI’s sole geographic decision mechanism.

When IR authority is withdrawn, NAVI uses applicable historical MM-to-MM arrival times as its comparison reference.

There is no separate timer-based acceptance gate.

If an observation is more consistent with MM+2 than MM+1, NAVI records the missed MM, stops, and notifies the operator.

If NAVI cannot obtain sufficient Hall evidence to maintain navigation, it stops and notifies the operator.

Do not manufacture Hall observations or geographic advancement.

6. Field instrumentation

Record every IR credibility transition with:

* Current and expected MM.
* Selected Hall opening ID.
* Expected interval distance.
* Measured interval distance.
* Absolute and signed discrepancy.
* IR pulse counts and measurement provenance.
* Current IR authority state.
* Withdrawal or restoration reason.
* Geographic decision and selected measurement method.

Preserve the observations needed to evaluate whether the provisional criteria are appropriate.

7. Mandatory reassessment

Both numerical criteria must be revisited after EWO _0_4 begins producing field data.

Specifically:

* The 10 mm inconsistency criterion.
* The five-pulse withdrawal criterion.

The earlier September 28 data included significant packet-association uncertainty. That limitation has since been addressed in the experimental implementation, but its improvement must be verified in actual operation.

Do not treat the provisional values as permanent physical laws or modify them automatically during operation.

8. Implementation boundaries

Implement these criteria in the experimental EWO _0_4 navigation system.

Preserve:

* 70×2 Hall detection.
* Closest-to-target geographic selection.
* The original 171-interval map.
* Existing IR calibration.
* The TILE/STOP OVERLAY architecture.
* Existing motor-control and electrical protections.

Do not reinstate the ±15% gate, the 650 ms fallback, or automatic Hall-free geographic advancement.

Do not change the numerical criteria without operator approval.

Continue measuring IR while authority is withdrawn.

Geographic knowledge governs trust in the measurement—not the other way around.
