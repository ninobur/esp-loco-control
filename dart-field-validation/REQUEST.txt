1. Governing objective

NAVI must identify one qualifying geographic landmark at each expected mile-marker position.

The navigation process is:

Know target → Detect → Qualify → Compare → Select → Advance.

The central innovation is the dart model: select the qualifying Hall observation closest to the expected geographic target.

The model operates using either IR-measured distance or historically observed arrival time.

The old ±15% distance gate and 650 ms fallback are eliminated completely.

There is no automatic advancement without a qualifying Hall observation.

2. Complete the existing DART implementation

Continue from the implemented and host-tested NAVI_EWO_0_1_DART_EXPERIMENT_1.

Do not start another architectural redesign or discard working code.

Preserve:

* Median-of-five, 70×2 Hall detection.
* Successive elimination of candidates.
* Closest-to-target comparison.
* Immediate selection of a better qualifying candidate beyond the expected target.
* Symmetrical completion when no better candidate appears.
* Midpoint handling of equally qualified, equally distant candidates.
* Consistent evaluated and saved IR coordinates.
* Preserved observation and decision evidence.
* One geographic advancement per selected target.

Do not introduce morphology-based exclusions.

Do not reinstate percentage-based distance gates.

3. IR credibility

Implement NAVI’s established evidence-based judgment.

IR distance is useful when it is consistent with NAVI’s accumulated geographic knowledge.

A magnetic encounter that is otherwise well supported must not be discarded merely because IR reports an unexpectedly short interval.

NAVI should distinguish:

A. Normal agreement

The Hall observation, geographic sequence, and IR distance are consistent.

Continue normal navigation.

B. Isolated discrepancy

A qualifying geographic observation disagrees with IR distance.

Preserve the observation and discrepancy. Continue evaluating subsequent evidence.

C. Repeated discrepancy

Successive observations demonstrate that IR distance is no longer consistent with geographic reality.

Withdraw IR’s authority for geographic selection, report the condition, and use historical timing.

D. Restoration

When subsequent evidence establishes that IR measurements have become consistent again, restore IR geographic authority.

Do not treat a functioning packet stream as proof of accurate distance measurement.

Do not invent arbitrary percentage gates.

Use the existing accumulated observations, local and cumulative distance relationships, and documented operating context.

If a numerical decision parameter is unavoidable, identify it explicitly and justify it from available evidence. Do not silently introduce one.

4. Historical timing — the ticker tape

Implement the agreed historical timing mechanism as NAVI’s alternative geographic measurement.

This is not PWM-based distance estimation.

NAVI records actual elapsed times between identified magnetic landmarks.

Maintain bounded records indexed by geographic interval and direction.

Use recent MM-to-MM timing for ordinary running.

For station approaches, use previous successful traversal timing, including the elapsed time from throttle application following a station dwell.

Preserve relevant stop, restart, and operating context.

When IR is unavailable or has lost geographic credibility:

1. Establish the expected arrival time of the next MM from applicable historical observations.
2. Qualify Hall observations normally.
3. Compare their arrival times against the expected target time.
4. Select the closest qualifying observation using the same dart model.
5. Advance once.

Do not use a fixed 650 ms fallback.

Do not implement PWM ticker-tape playback or closed-loop speed regulation.

If no suitable historical timing record exists, report that limitation rather than inventing a prediction.

5. Missing-magnet recognition

Implement our agreed MM+1 versus MM+2 comparison.

Suppose NAVI has identified MM090 and expects MM091.

It knows the expected geographic positions of both MM091 and MM092.

If a qualifying observation is closer to the expected position of MM092 than MM091, using the applicable distance or timing reference, NAVI concludes:

MM091 was missed. The observation is more consistent with MM092. Stop and notify the operator.

This applies even when MM091 and MM092 have the same polarity.

Requirements:

* Do not manufacture a Hall observation for MM091.
* Do not silently advance through the missing target.
* Preserve the actual observed evidence supporting MM092.
* Initiate a controlled stop.
* Report the missed target and observed geographic interpretation.
* Require appropriate operator action before resuming.

If Hall observations cease and NAVI cannot maintain geographic navigation, stop and notify the operator.

Do not treat an ordinary interval between magnets as a sensor failure. Use the expected geographic progression and available movement evidence.

6. Geographic reference

Retain the existing 171-interval map for the initial field experiment.

Do not automatically replace it with measurements from a single lap.

The recent stability study found that individual Hall-to-Hall measurements vary because of pulse quantization, packet association, and other factors.

The full-circuit IR measurement was highly repeatable, but local measurement accuracy remains less certain.

Collect actual Hall-opening-to-Hall-opening distances during field operation.

Preserve the distinction between:

* Physical disc-magnet positions.
* Hall magnetic-field opening positions.
* IR cumulative coordinates.
* NAVI’s selected geographic identities.

Do not modify map calibration or stopping geometry during this experiment.

7. Preserve TILE and STOP OVERLAY

Preserve the existing twelve-tile rainbow-carpet architecture.

The STOP OVERLAY is geographically anchored to the designated stopping tile and extends backward across eleven preceding tiles.

Do not modify its established PWM settings, ramps, braking, dwell, departure, or stopping geometry.

Preserve manual control, direction handling, STOP/GO, E-stop, and electrical protections.

NAVI must supply selected geographic identity independently of fine IR availability.

Verify that the OVERLAY receives the correct tile information and does not manufacture a fine-position measurement when IR is unavailable.

8. Field-test instrumentation

The working sketch must produce sufficient telemetry to explain its geographic decisions after a run.

For each Hall observation, record:

* Hall opening serial and timestamp.
* ADC reference, polarity, and qualification.
* Expected MM identity and polarity.
* Expected distance or arrival time.
* Measured IR distance and validity, when available.
* Historical timing prediction, when applicable.
* Candidate comparison and elimination.
* Selected target and decision boundary.
* Geographic advancement or missed-target judgment.
* IR credibility status and reason for changes.
* Current geographic anchor.
* Relevant PWM, direction, stop, and restart context.

Preserve the existing native Hall and IR recording facilities.

Ensure that diagnostic recording cannot block Hall acquisition, motor control, or electrical protection.

Verify that records actually reach the Pi recorder. Successful MQTT publication alone is insufficient.

9. Controlled field-test sequence

Prepare the sketch for a staged test on Otto.

Stage A — Bench verification

Compile with ESP32 core 3.3.12.

Verify memory headroom, Hall sampling timing, candidate processing, queue behavior, and MQTT recording.

Test the motor and STOP OVERLAY interfaces without operating the locomotive on the railroad.

Stage B — Initial geographic trial

With the operator supervising:

1. Declare the initial geographic interval.
2. Run a short controlled section containing several magnets.
3. Verify that NAVI identifies the expected sequence.
4. Confirm that selected Hall coordinates are preserved.
5. Verify that repeated openings do not produce duplicate advancement.
6. Inspect recorded decisions before extending the trial.

Stage C — Full clockwise circuit

If Stage B succeeds, conduct a complete clockwise circuit.

Compare NAVI’s reported MM sequence against independently observed physical landmarks.

Record Hall-to-Hall distances, decision latency, IR credibility judgments, and any unresolved events.

Stage D — Repeated circuit

Run a second clockwise circuit under comparable conditions.

Measure the repeatability of Hall-opening distances and arrival times.

Evaluate whether the historical timing system produces useful predictions.

Stage E — Controlled degraded-IR evaluation

Only after ordinary navigation is demonstrated, evaluate the no-IR timing mode under controlled conditions.

Verify that it can identify successive magnets without the historical 650 ms fallback.

Do not deliberately create unsafe track conditions or disable electrical protections.

Governing principle

NAVI is not navigating an unfamiliar railroad.

It knows the map, recognizes magnetic landmarks, and accumulates experience from repeated journeys.

Its job is to use that knowledge to make defensible geographic decisions.

One target. One qualifying selection. One advancement.
